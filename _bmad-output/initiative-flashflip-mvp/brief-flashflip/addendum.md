# Addendum: FlashFlip

## Competitive landscape (web research, 2026-10-28)

Sources are mostly third-party reviews; vendor pricing pages not checked. Treat figures as approximate.

**Gap observed:** no tool found that is zero-signup, ad-free, non-AI and uncapped, and that takes the student's own term–definition pairs straight into a scored self-test.

| Tool | Friction for a first-year student |
|---|---|
| Quizlet | Account needed to save sets; ads; free Learn/Test limited (reportedly ~5 Learn rounds, 1 practice test per set); Plus ~$7.99/mo. Import supports a custom separator, so FlashFlip's `term - definition` format likely works (unconfirmed). |
| Anki | Free on desktop/web/Android, $24.99 on iOS; steep learning curve. |
| Knowt | Free, AI-heavy, capped AI use; login requirement unverified. |
| Brainscape | Free tier pushes upgrades; Pro ~$4.99–9.99/mo; free limits unverified. |
| Gizmo | App only, AI-first; 15 "lives"/day on free; unlimited plan ~$13.99/week; no offline mode. |
| flashcards.world / Yippity / Slidespeak | No signup, but AI-generated cards; ~10-card caps; saving needs an account (vendor claims, unverified). |

## Counter-arguments and risks

Alternatives students already have:
- Quizlet's basic flip mode is free and familiar.
- Paste-to-card sites exist.
- Students can ask a chatbot to quiz them.

FlashFlip's own limits:
- No spaced repetition.
- Clearing browser storage deletes the deck; no cross-device use.

## Learning-science evidence

The studies below support building a scored self-test rather than a flip-only review.

- Dunlosky et al. (2013), *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*: practice testing and distributed practice were the only two of 10 techniques rated "high utility"; rereading and highlighting were rated low.
- Roediger & Karpicke (2006): retrieval practice gave ~61% retention after a week vs ~40% for rereading (secondary source; verify before citing).
